# Every ordering occurs for G-quadratic Artinian Gorenstein algebras

**Theorem.** Fix any field, an integer $d\ge 3$, and a permutation $\pi$ of $\{1,\ldots,\lfloor d/2\rfloor\}$. There exists a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra of socle degree $d$, with a quadratic Gröbner basis, whose Hilbert function satisfies
$$
H_{\pi(1)}<H_{\pi(2)}<\cdots<H_{\pi(\lfloor d/2\rfloor)}.
$$
A quadratic Gröbner basis implies Koszulness. This proves the published form of Conjectures 1.4 and 5.8 of Holleben and Nicklasson. Their Theorem 5.7 supplies the permutations without the Koszul condition. The published conjecture uses the floor, including for odd $d$. [Holleben and Nicklasson, *Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra* 230 (2026), 108238](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpaa.2026.108238)

## From colored complexes to algebras

Write $p=d-1$ and $m=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$. If $\Gamma$ is a flag simplicial complex with a proper coloring by $p$ colors, let $q_s$ count its faces of size $s$, including $q_0=1$. Add one vertex $y_i$ for each color. A face of the new complex $\Delta$ consists of a face $F$ of $\Gamma$ and any subset of the new vertices whose colors are absent from $F$. This is the colored whiskering of Biermann and Van Tuyl. Its facets contain exactly one vertex of each color, and the complex is shellable. It is flag because a set of pairwise compatible vertices has an old part that is a face of $\Gamma$, and each new vertex avoids all old vertices of its color. [Biermann and Van Tuyl, Theorem 3.5 and Corollary 3.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.0044)

Let $F(x)$ count faces of $\Delta$ by size. Every old $s$-face has $p-s$ available new vertices, so
$$
 F(x)=\sum_{s=0}^p q_s x^s(1+x)^{p-s}.
$$
Here is an explicit presentation of the algebra over the chosen field $K$. Give $\Delta$ one variable $u_a$ for each vertex $a$ and one variable $z_T$ for each facet $T$, all of degree one. Write $u^S=\prod_{a\in S}u_a$. Take its quotient by the ideal generated by the following elements.

- $u_a^2$ for each vertex and $u_a u_b$ for each nonedge $\{a,b\}$ of $\Delta$.
- $z_Tz_U$ for all pairs of facets, including $T=U$.
- $u_a z_T$ for $a\notin T$.
- $u^{T\setminus U}z_T-u^{U\setminus T}z_U$ for pairs of distinct facets.

This is D’Alì and Venturello’s $R_\Delta/(y_a-x_a:a\in V(\Delta))$, obtained by substituting $y_a=x_a=u_a$ in their Proposition 4.3. They prove that the linear forms being factored out are a regular sequence, that the quotient is Artinian Gorenstein, and that a pure flag shellable $\Delta$ gives it a quadratic Gröbner basis. Their Remark 8.4 explicitly extends these statements for the linear reduction to arbitrary characteristic. [D’Alì and Venturello, Proposition 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.05051v2#S4) · [Propositions 8.1 and 8.3, Remark 8.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.05051v2#S8)

The Hilbert series of this Artinian reduction is $F(x)+x^{p+1}F(1/x)$. This is Remark 4.2 of D’Alì and Venturello together with the invariance of the $h$-polynomial under quotient by a regular linear form. It can also be read from the presentation. Monomials without facet variables are indexed by faces. Each surviving monomial with one facet variable has the form $u^{T\setminus S}z_T$ for a face $S\subseteq T$, and lies in degree $p+1-|S|$. The binomials identify exactly those monomials with the same $S$: multiplying the relation between facets $T,U$ by $u^{(T\cap U)\setminus S}$ gives the identification, and all surviving terms of each multiple have the same remaining face. Thus the degree-$k$ dimension counts faces of sizes $k$ and $d-k$. [D’Alì and Venturello, Remark 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.05051v2#S4)

## A flat polynomial with independently adjustable middle coefficients

For $s\ge 1$, put $i=p-s$. The contribution from one $s$-face to $H_k$ is
$$
 B_i(k)=\binom{i}{k-1}+\binom{i}{p-k},
 \qquad 1\le k\le m.
$$
Here a binomial coefficient vanishes when its lower entry exceeds the upper. The empty face contributes $\binom{d}{k}$.

The positive integers $q_s^0=2^{s-1}$, $1\le s\le p$, make the nonempty-face contribution constant at every interior degree. Indeed
$$
 F_0(x)=\sum_{s=1}^p2^{s-1}x^s(1+x)^{p-s}
       =x\frac{(1+x)^p-(2x)^p}{1-x}.
$$
Consequently
$$
 F_0(x)+x^{p+1}F_0(1/x)=2^p(x+x^2+\cdots+x^p).
$$

The $m$ vectors $B_0,\ldots,B_{m-1}$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$. With rows $1\le k\le m$ and columns $0\le i<m$, entries below the diagonal vanish because $i<k-1\le p-k$. On the diagonal the first term is one. The second contributes one only in the final row when $d$ is even. Given $y_k=\pi^{-1}(k)$, solve $\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_i B_i(k)=y_k$ over $\mathbb Q$. Extend $c_i$ by zero for $m\le i<p$. Choose a positive rational $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small that
$$
 q_s=2^{s-1}+\varepsilon c_{p-s}>0\quad(1\le s\le p).
$$
The nonempty-face contribution of these rational targets is exactly $2^p+\varepsilon y_k$.

## Realizing the positive targets by an actual flag complex

Any positive rational list $q_1,\ldots,q_p$ occurs, up to a common positive integer factor, as the counts of the nonempty faces of a $p$-colored flag complex. To see this, put $P(x)=\sum_{s=1}^p q_s x^s$ and expand $P((z-1)/N)=\sum_{j=0}^p b_j z^j$. For $j\ge1$,
$$
 b_j=\sum_{s=j}^{p}(-1)^{s-j}\binom{s}{j}\frac{q_s}{N^s}.
$$
As $N\to\infty$, $N^j b_j\to q_j>0$, so one integer $N$ makes all $b_j$ with $j\ge1$ positive. Since $P(0)=0$, $b_0=-\sum_{j=1}^p b_j$. Choose a common denominator $M$ for $b_1,\ldots,b_p$ and form the disjoint union, over $1\le j\le p$, of $Mb_j$ copies of the clique complex of the complete $j$-partite graph with $N$ vertices per part. In each copy use the first $j$ colors on its parts. Its nonempty-face polynomial is $(1+Nx)^j-1$. The nonempty-face polynomial of the union is therefore
$$
 M\sum_{j=1}^p b_j\big((1+Nx)^j-1\big)=MP(x).
$$
The union and its components are flag, and $b_p>0$ ensures that all $p$ colors occur.

Applying the colored whiskering and Artinian reduction gives
$$
 H_k=\binom{d}{k}+M(2^p+\varepsilon\,\pi^{-1}(k)),
 \qquad 1\le k\le m.
$$
Any positive multiple of the denominator $M$ still realizes the target counts, so choose $M\varepsilon>\max_k\binom{d}{k}$. This proves the prescribed strict inequalities. The construction also yields arbitrarily large consecutive gaps in any prescribed order.

## Sources and status

The published question appears in Holleben and Nicklasson's 2026 paper and its 2025 preprint. Ke-kuk Chow’s manuscript dated November 6, 2026 covers the separate weak Lefschetz conjecture for paired truncations. The November 25, 2026 Evidence Press manuscript also addresses Conjecture 3.15 on the weak Lefschetz property of a whiskered graph algebra, including a classification for idealizations of ordinary whiskerings. The argument here uses color classes of arbitrary size. The MathDB entry on the Koszul Roller Coaster conjecture showed no recorded solution when checked. These are distinct claims, and the searches do not certify priority.
